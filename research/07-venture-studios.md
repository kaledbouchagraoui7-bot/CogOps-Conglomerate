# Case 07 — Venture Studios · Idealab, Rocket Internet, Atomic — The Company Factory

**Archetype C (company factory).** Chosen because studios are the only organizations whose *product is companies*: they solved idea-funnel economics, shared-build teams, staged de-risking, and early killing — the exact problems CogOps faces at birth-stage. Three studios, three different lessons: **Idealab** (funnel + timing), **Rocket Internet** (replication speed + its limits), **Atomic** (small-team build → CEO spin-out).

## 1. Scale facts (as-of dated)

|---|---|---|

## 2. Mechanism analysis (M1–M12) — the shared studio pattern, then per-studio deviations

**M1 · Topology.** Studio-at-center, companies-at-edge: a permanent small organization (partners + shared build team + platform services) that spawns legally independent companies holding studio equity. Companies are *born* from the studio (vs. acquired by holdcos).

**M2 · Decision rights.** During incubation the studio holds all rights (it is the founder); at spin-out, rights transfer to the company's recruited CEO/founding team — the studio retains board seat(s), equity, and preferred-services relationships, not operational control.

**M3 · Capital allocation — staged de-risking.** Capital is released in small tranches against evidence: idea → desk research → prototype → user test → company formation → external round. Idealab's funnel ratio (**5,000 ideas → 150 companies ≈ 3% survival**) is the discipline made visible: the studio's core asset is its *rejection machinery*. Gross's own analysis (TED, 2015): of the five success factors, **timing** mattered most — studios win by testing many timings cheaply.

**M4 · Unit lifecycle — the canonical studio pipeline.**
```
ideation (internal + inbound, thousands) → screen (market/timing/team-fit)
→ validation (weeks: landing pages, concierge tests, pre-sales) → BUILD (shared team, months)
→ company formation (legal entity, brand, recruited CEO/founding team)
→ external capital + independence → studio portfolio support → exit (IPO/trade) or shutdown
```
Kills concentrate early (validation) where cost ≈ weeks of shared-team time. Rocket deviation: skip ideation — *clone proven models* (validation risk already retired by the original), compete on **execution speed** ("land-and-expand market by market in months"). Atomic deviation: build deeper in-house (product + early team), then recruit an external CEO and spin out with venture financing.

**M5 · Autonomy contract.** Post-spin: full autonomy; the studio's continuing leverage is reputation, follow-on capital, and shared-services pricing — not control. Rocket's centralized control during scale-up (central functions directed markets) is the deviation that its later struggles are attributed to in post-mortems.

**M6 · Shared platform.** The build engine: shared developers/designers/growth operators deployed across incubations; legal/finance/HR/recruiting as services; reusable tech templates. Rocket industrialized this (central "academy" trained launch teams deployed country-by-country); Idealab's shared "P-suite" of executive services; Atomic's in-house founding teams.

**M7 · Cadence.** Weekly incubation reviews (kill/scale/pivot decisions); milestone-based tranche releases; portfolio-level quarterly capital reviews. Rocket: war-room launch cadence (multi-market landings in parallel).

**M8 · Metrics.** Validation-stage: signups, waitlist conversion, pre-order revenue, CAC proxies — *evidence metrics*, not revenue. Company-stage: standard startup metrics. Studio-level: portfolio IRR, capital recycled, kill-rate health (too low = not killing; too high = wrong funnel).

**M9 · Talent.** Studio keeps a bench of entrepreneurs-in-residence / founding teams; the pivotal act is **CEO selection at spin-out** (Atomic's signature move; Idealab often installs EIRs earlier). Compensation: equity in the spun company.

**M10 · Culture.** Playbook transmission: Rocket's operating manuals (documented launch playbooks per model) and Idealab's idea-evaluation heuristics are the studios' DBS-analogues. Culture risk noted in Rocket post-mortems: speed-over-quality eroded brands — a transmission failure of *standards* (they transmitted process, not taste).

**M11 · Risk.** Tranche caps bound downside per bet; portfolio math justifies individual failures; legal separation prevents contagion; kill authority is pre-committed (the funnel IS the risk system). Rocket's counter-lesson: replication risk (legal/ethical exposure, brand damage) is real when speed outruns standards.

**M12 · Compounding.** Exits recycle capital into the funnel; alumni networks feed deal flow and teams; playbook improves per iteration (Idealab over 30 years: 5,000 ideas of institutional learning). The studio compounds *optionality*, not just equity.

## 3. Adopt / Adapt / Reject for CogOps

| Decision | Mechanism | Studio element | CogOps translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADOPT** | M4 | The canonical pipeline (idea → screen → validate → build → company → external capital → exit/kill) | Directly instantiated as OPERATING-STRUCTURE §5 Stages 0–5 — the lifecycle backbone, fused with Alphabet's gates |
| **ADOPT** | M3 | Funnel ratio discipline (~3% idea→company) + tranche releases | Stage envelopes in CHF + evidence gates; the idea funnel is fed continuously by Growth (SEO/market signals) and Talent (skill-gap data) |
| **ADOPT** | M8 | Evidence metrics pre-revenue; kill-rate as a portfolio health metric | Stage 0–1 metrics = validation evidence (waitlists, pre-sales, pilot LOIs); quarterly kill-rate review: a portfolio that never kills is failing the funnel |
| **ADOPT** | M9 | CEO-at-spin-out | At Stage 4, each subsidiary gets a named accountable lead — agent-squad "CEO role" today; first human GM hired per the studio's selection rigor (Bar-Raiser adapted, Amazon M9) |
| **ADAPT** | M6 | Shared build team | The shared build team is the **agent hierarchy + site factory**: a new company = KB + PR/FAQ + brand config + product catalog deployed on the platform — days, not months |
| **ADAPT** | M10 | Playbooks | Merged into CBS (Case 05) — one system, studio pipeline included as CBS chapter "Birth" |
| **REJECT** | — | Rocket's clone-at-any-speed model | Standards over speed (the blueprint's evidence discipline); replication *of proven models* remains allowed (it's how Stage-0 ideas may be sourced) but never at the cost of the compliance/verification gates |
| **REJECT** | M3 | Studio economics requiring 10–30 bets/yr | Solo+AI bandwidth: ≤ 2–3 active incubations per quarter; funnel stays wide (ideas cheap to screen), *build slots* stay narrow |
