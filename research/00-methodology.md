# Research Methodology — How These Benchmarks Were Chosen and Analyzed

> Part of the CogOps-Conglomerate research corpus. Reading order: `00-methodology` → `01…08` (cases) → `09` (Swiss legal reality) → `10` (synthesis) → `../structure/OPERATING-STRUCTURE.md` (the deliverable).

## 1. The question being answered

The blueprint (`../README.md`) defines **what** CogOps Group is: one central AI-operated business, ten domain companies, deep-tech excluded. This corpus answers **how**: which existing organizations have already solved the structural problems of running many companies under one center, exactly how they work, and which of their mechanisms CogOps copies, adapts, or rejects.

## 2. Benchmark selection

No single company is a perfect analogue of an AI-operated micro-conglomerate — so instead of copying one model, we decompose the problem into **four archetypes**, each holding the world's best-proven answer to one part of it:

| Archetype | The problem it solves | Benchmarks | Why the best |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Decentralized capital allocators** | How a center controls capital without controlling operations | Berkshire Hathaway · Constellation Software · Tiny | The three most consistently compounding owner-operators of many small/medium businesses; each ran/runs dozens-to-hundreds of units with tiny headquarters |
| **B. Platform holdings** | How subsidiaries get independence + shared leverage, and how they are born/graduated/killed | Alphabet Inc. · Haier (Rendanheyi) | Alphabet = the canonical tech holding (Other Bets lifecycle); Haier = the most radical "many micro-companies on one platform" model ever operated at Fortune-Global-500 scale |
| **C. Company factories & operating systems** | How to *manufacture* companies repeatably, and how one playbook runs across all of them | Amazon (mechanisms) · Danaher (DBS) · Idealab · Rocket Internet · Atomic | Amazon = operating mechanisms (APIs, narratives, input metrics); Danaher = a deployable business system; the studios = pipeline economics of idea → company → exit/kill |
| **D. Solo/micro portfolios** | What one person can actually run, with AI leverage, today | Pieter Levels' portfolio · Tiny (again, at small scale) | The only *empirically verified* one-person multi-company operations with public numbers |

**Excluded from benchmarking on purpose:** SoftBank/Vision Fund and PE holdcos (capital-deployment vehicles, not operators); 3M/GE (conglomerates whose diversity became a discount — the "conglomerate discount" is itself a lesson, captured in the synthesis); franchise systems (no central intelligence).

## 3. The extraction frame — 12 mechanisms

Every case file analyzes the company through the same 12-mechanism taxonomy, so the cases are comparable cell-by-cell and the synthesis can be built as a matrix rather than vibes:

| # | Mechanism | The structural question it answers |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Structural topology | How are units arranged under the center? Legal vs. operational vs. brand structure |
| M2 | Decision rights & governance | Who decides what? Which decisions are reversible? Where are the gates? |
| M3 | Capital allocation | How does money flow to, between, and out of units? What are the hurdles? |
| M4 | Unit lifecycle | How is a unit born, funded through stages, scaled, spun out — or killed? |
| M5 | Autonomy contract | What does the center *never* touch? What does the unit *never* decide? |
| M6 | Shared platform | What common services/infrastructure does the center provide? |
| M7 | Operating cadence & reporting | What rhythms (reviews, narratives, letters) keep the center informed without micromanaging? |
| M8 | Metrics philosophy | Input vs. output metrics; what is measured weekly vs. annually |
| M9 | Talent & leadership model | How are unit leaders found, incentivized, replaced? |
| M10 | Culture transmission | How do principles survive replication across units and time? |
| M11 | Risk management & kill criteria | What stops a unit or an action? Fail-safe vs. fail-closed |
| M12 | Compounding & reinvestment | Where do profits go? How does the system get stronger per cycle? |

For each case: **Structure** (the facts, sourced), then **Adopt / Adapt / Reject** decisions for CogOps, then **Sources**. The adoption decisions are collected into the master ledger in `10-cross-case-synthesis.md` §3 and instantiated in `../structure/OPERATING-STRUCTURE.md` §13.

## 4. Evidence discipline (anti-snowball rules)

Because a small factual error here would propagate into every structure decision downstream, this corpus follows hard rules:

1. **As-of dating.** Every quantitative claim carries a date ("as of FY2025", "as of 2026-03"). Numbers are treated as perishable; mechanisms are treated as durable.
2. **Primary-source preference.** Owner letters, annual reports, official IR pages and the companies' own documentation first; reputable secondary analysis second; podcasts/interviews third (marked as such).
3. **No fabricated precision.** Where sources disagree or a number could not be re-verified in this research pass, it is written as approximate (`~`) or omitted — an honest gap beats a precise falsehood.
4. **Separation of fact and judgment.** Case files state what the company *does*; adoption decisions state what CogOps *should copy* — never mixed in the same sentence.
5. **Traceability.** Every structural element of the operating structure document cites the mechanism (M#) and the benchmark it was copied/adapted from (§13 Adoption Ledger). Nothing in `structure/` exists without a lineage entry.

## 5. What "copy the structure" means here

CogOps cannot copy Berkshire's float or Alphabet's balance sheet — it copies their **mechanisms at solo+AI scale**: the decision rules, the contracts, the cadences, the lifecycle gates, the capital hurdles. Where a mechanism depended on scale (e.g., external capital markets, thousands of managers), the adaptation is explicit and reasoned, never silent. The scale translation table lives in `10-cross-case-synthesis.md` §4.
