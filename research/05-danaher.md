# Case 05 — Danaher · The Deployable Business System (DBS)

**Archetype C (operating system).** Chosen because Danaher answers the question every multi-company operator eventually faces: *how does one way of working survive replication across dozens of businesses?* Answer: DBS — a codified, teachable, deployable business system that travels with every acquisition and every leader. For CogOps (where the "leaders" are agent squads and later hired GMs), a written deployable system is not optional — it is the entire management layer.

## 2. Mechanism analysis (M1–M12)

**M1 · Structural topology.** Holding → operating companies (each a portfolio of businesses) → businesses. Structurally ordinary; the differentiator is the **horizontal layer**: DBS is neither a department nor a subsidiary — it is an operating system owned by the center and *instantiated inside every unit*.

**M2 · Decision rights.** Units run their P&Ls with DBS as the common language of *how*; the center owns portfolio boundaries (enter/exit industries), capital, CEO selection, and — uniquely — **ownership of the system itself** (the DBS Office curates, updates and certifies the playbook; changes propagate like software releases).

**M3 · Capital allocation.** Buy-and-improve: acquire businesses with improvable economics (often family-owned, under-invested), deploy DBS to lift margins/growth (funnel management, pricing, voice-of-customer), reinvest or exit entire industries when better uses appear (the great pivot from diversified industrials → science & tech, executed via Fortive/Envista/Veralto spin-offs). Capital follows *system-fit*: DBS works best where processes can be standardized — the portfolio is selected for applicability of the system.

**M4 · Unit lifecycle.** Acquire → **DBS immersion** (acquired leadership undergoes intensive multi-day DBS training — documented as a three-day intensive on acquisition; kaizen events within first months) → improve → grow → retain or spin off at portfolio level. Birth = acquisition; graduation = spin-off into its own listed company with DBS DNA intact (Fortive and Veralto both run DBS-descendant systems post-spin).

**M5 · Autonomy contract.** Operational autonomy inside the system: unit leaders choose *what* to improve and run their markets, but the *grammar* (how planning, problem-solving, daily management, customer voice work) is non-negotiable DBS.

**M6 · Shared platform.** DBS tools (Policy Deployment, Value Stream Mapping, Standard Work, Problem Solving Process/PSP, Funnel Management, Value Selling, Daily Management, Transactional Process Improvement, Leadership Standards), the DBS Office (central curation), and **DBS Leaders (DBSLs)** — dedicated deployed experts per company who coach rather than command.

**M7 · Cadence.** Policy Deployment (hoshin kanri): annual breakthrough objectives cascaded with catchball; daily management boards at team level; monthly PQLs (process-quality-leadership reviews) per business; leadership standard work (executives' own routines documented and audited).

**M8 · Metrics — the 4P frame.** **People, Plan, Process, Performance**: people metrics (retention, internal promotion, DBS proficiency), plan metrics (policy-deployment achievement), process metrics (cycle time, quality, funnel conversion), performance metrics (revenue growth, margin, FCF). Performance is explicitly *downstream* of the other three — the system measures its own inputs.

**M9 · Talent.** The leadership pipeline is DBS itself: leaders are trained via immersion, proved by running kaizen/PD cycles, promoted for DBS fluency + results; CEO candidates historically emerged from DBS-minted operator ranks. "The best team wins" is the talent slogan — teams, not individuals.

**M10 · Culture transmission.** DBS is culture-as-artifact: written tools, certified training, leadership standard work, rituals (kaizen events), and stories (the 1980s kaizen origin). Because it is *taught* rather than absorbed, it survived 40 years, two founder generations, and 3+ major spin-offs — the strongest evidence in this corpus that a written operating system outlives its authors.

**M11 · Risk.** Portfolio risk managed by system-fit selection + industry exits; operational risk by standard work + escalation (PSP triggers when metrics breach); the spin-offs prevent complexity risk (a company too big for the system gets its own listing and its own DBS lineage).

**M12 · Compounding.** DBS itself compounds: every kaizen improves the playbook; every acquisition adds patterns; every leader trained multiplies deployment capacity. The flywheel: system → better units → more FCF → more acquisitions → more system material.

## 3. Adopt / Adapt / Reject for CogOps

| Decision | Mechanism | Danaher element | CogOps translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADOPT** | M10 | Codified deployable business system | **CBS — the CogOps Business System** (OPERATING-STRUCTURE §10): one written playbook (validation protocol, FIXES-style verification discipline, KB-first grounding, narrative reviews, gate protocol, kill protocol) instantiated in every subsidiary; versioned like software |
| **ADOPT** | M4 | Immersion on entry | Every new agent squad / future human GM "onboards" by loading CBS + subsidiary KB before acting — the 3-day immersion at machine speed |
| **ADOPT** | M8 | 4P metrics (People, Plan, Process, Performance) | Subsidiary scorecards measure **Squad, Plan, Process, Performance** in that order — performance downstream of inputs (fuses with Amazon M8) |
| **ADOPT** | M6 | DBSL coaches (deployed, non-commanding) | The **Auditing Agent** role = standing DBSL: reviews every subsidiary against CBS periodically, coaches via findings, never executes in the unit |
| **ADAPT** | M7 | Policy Deployment + daily boards | Quarterly Policy-Deployment-lite: 1–2 breakthrough objectives per subsidiary per quarter; weekly metric sheet (Amazon WBR) as the "daily board" |
| **ADAPT** | M3 | Buy-and-improve | Shelved with Constellation's playbook as the **Phase-2 acquisition doctrine**: when the sweep portfolio funds purchases, CBS is the improvement engine (Danaher proves the system transfers to acquired units) |
| **REJECT** | M1 | Multi-layer holding (Danaher Corp → opcos → businesses) | Two layers only (Group → subsidiaries) until a subsidiary itself needs sub-units |
| **REJECT** | M7 | Full hoshin catchball ceremonies | One founder: catchball = founder ↔ agent-squad proposal/review loop inside the gate, not a meeting calendar |
