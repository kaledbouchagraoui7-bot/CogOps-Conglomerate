# Case 06 — Haier · Rendanheyi — Thousands of Micro-Companies on One Platform

**Archetype B (platform holding).** Chosen because Haier is the most radical large-scale proof that the *company-as-monolith* can be dissolved into **market-facing micro-enterprises (MEs)** sharing one platform — the exact topology CogOps needs, at a scale that proves it isn't a boutique theory. For an AI-operated group, Haier matters twice: its MEs are what agent squads imitate (bounded, market-facing, self-measuring), and its platform is what the CogOps AI core imitates.

## 2. Mechanism analysis (M1–M12)

**M1 · Structural topology.** Inverted pyramid: **platform at the center, MEs at the edge facing users.** The group is not a hierarchy but an *investment platform + shared services* hosting thousands of MEs (each ~10–30 people), organized into loosely-coupled micro-communities (ecosystem nodes, e.g., "smart kitchen" community). MEs form around user needs and dissolve when the need is served or the bet fails. Zero distance to users: no layer may stand between an ME and its users.

**M2 · Decision rights — the "three rights."** Each ME holds: **decision rights** (what to build/sell, pricing within bounds), **hiring rights** (recruit/release its own members), **compensation rights** (set its own reward split from ME profit). The platform retains: brand standards, capital access terms, compliance, and platform-service pricing. Middle managers were removed because their coordination decisions were replaced by user-pay signals.

**M3 · Capital allocation.** MEs propose business plans ("bet-on agreements", VAM-style): the platform funds against milestones; **MEs may raise external VC** while Haier retains equity — the platform acts as anchor investor, not sole wallet. Surplus profit is shared with ME members (the "shengyu fenhong" mechanism): members' upside is literally paid by users via ME profit, not by HQ salary. Underperforming MEs lose funding and dissolve; successful MEs scale into micro-communities or IPO (Haier Biomedical).

**M4 · Unit lifecycle.** Opportunity detected (by anyone, anywhere) → ME proposal (leader + plan + target users) → platform approval + seed capital → operate with three rights → scale (attract external capital, spawn sub-MEs) → graduate (own legal entity/listing) or dissolve (members return to the talent market). Birth and death are *continuous and cheap* — the platform expects most MEs to fail small.

**M5 · Autonomy contract.** MEs are autonomous *in the market*, never autonomous *from users*: the "dan" (user need + willingness-to-pay target) is the contract. Miss the dan → dissolution. The platform's promise: services on demand, no operational commands.

**M6 · Shared platform.** Supply chain, manufacturing, logistics, HR/payroll, legal, finance, IT, brand, and the COSMOPlat industrial-internet layer — all offered to MEs as *pull services*. GE Appliances post-2016 is the proof case: US manufacturing giant converted to ME architecture on Haier's platform, returning to growth without HQ operational control (INSEAD case literature).

**M7 · Cadence.** No management meetings in the classic sense: cadence is market-driven — weekly/monthly user-pay results per ME, community-level coordination between MEs, platform-level capital reviews. Information flows through the platform's data systems, not reports up a chain.

**M8 · Metrics.** One metric family: **user value** — users served, willingness-to-pay realized, ME profit (from which members are paid), ecosystem growth. No internal transfer-pricing politics: if an ME needs another ME's capability, it buys it at market terms.

**M9 · Talent.** Leaders are not appointed — an ME leader emerges by proposing a dan others join; members choose their MEs (internal talent market); compensation is ME-profit-linked (base is minimal). Zhang Ruimin's formulation: "everyone their own CEO".

**M10 · Culture transmission.** The philosophy is written and taught globally (Rendanheyi Institute; INSEAD/HBR case canon: "Haier: No Middle Management, No Problems"-class literature); culture = the market mechanism itself (survival by user value, not politics). Language discipline: 人/单/合一 vocabulary forces every unit to define its user need explicitly.

**M11 · Risk.** Failure is contained by design: MEs are small, funded in tranches, dissolved early; the platform never concentrates risk in one ME; brand/compliance risk is centralized (platform standards). Systemic risk (platform itself) is managed by multi-listing and ecosystem diversification.

**M12 · Compounding.** Each successful ME compounds the platform (more services justified, more talent, more capital options); dissolutions free resources instantly; the ecosystem brand (Haier as "platform of platforms") attracts outside entrepreneurs to build ON it — compounding without central headcount growth.

## 3. Adopt / Adapt / Reject for CogOps

| Decision | Mechanism | Haier element | CogOps translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADOPT** | M1 | MEs face users; platform serves MEs; zero distance | Each CogOps subsidiary faces its market directly (own site, own CRM view, own inbox); the AI core is a platform, never a manager. No subsidiary may sell "internally" — internal work is billed at notional market terms so every company faces a real P&L |
| **ADOPT** | M2 | Three rights (decision/hiring/compensation) | Squad-level translation: each subsidiary squad holds decision rights within envelope, staffing rights over its agent roles (propose new agent roles from the 110-hierarchy), and earns budget-credibility from contribution |
| **ADOPT** | M4 | Cheap birth, cheap death; tranches | Stage envelopes + dissolution path (§5): failing companies dissolve in days, resources (agents, domains, KB) return to the platform pool |
| **ADOPT** | M8 | Users pay the metric | Subsidiary scorecards start from *user-paid value* (paid leads, retained clients, delivered artifacts) — never activity counts |
| **ADAPT** | M3 | Bet-on agreements + external VC per ME | Bet-on envelope contracts per subsidiary (founder funds vs. milestone); external capital only from Stage 4 (Alphabet-style), never earlier — solo-scale has no term-sheet bandwidth |
| **ADAPT** | M6 | Pull-only shared platform | Identical rule with teeth: platform services (site factory, gateway, CRM, legal templates) are pulled via tickets/requests; platform never pushes into subsidiaries |
| **ADAPT** | M9 | Leader emerges by dan | The "dan" is written for every subsidiary at launch (PR/FAQ + user-need statement per Amazon M4); squad-lead role is earned by proposal quality in the gate, not assigned by seniority |
| **REJECT** | M1 | Thousands of MEs / micro-communities | Scale is 10±3 subsidiaries; community layer activates only if a subsidiary spawns sub-brands |
| **REJECT** | M9 | Minimal base pay, profit-only compensation | Illegal/insane for humans at Swiss scale; reserved as an *agent* compensation analogue (compute budget follows contribution) |
| **REJECT** | M6 | Manufacturing/supply-chain platform | Software-only platform (already true in the blueprint's exclusion rule) |
